01Headline — 4/10

"B.Tech Student | Cloud & DevOps Intern | Full-Stack Developer | UI/UX Designer | Exploring AI, Cloud Technologies & Software Engineering | Building Real-World Projects"

problem This is six identities stapled together with pipes. Recruiter's 3-second read: "generalist, unsure what they actually do."

why it hurts LinkedIn search ranks headlines on relevance to a query, not keyword count. Claiming DevOps, full-stack, UI/UX, and AI all at once dilutes your match score for every one of them — you rank lower for "Cloud DevOps Intern" than a headline that commits to it.

invisible cost Recruiters searching for a specific skill (e.g. "Python Cloud Intern") scroll past profiles that read as scattered, even if you'd have been a good fit.

02About — first 2 lines — 3/10

"Hello, I'm Tarun Teja Patro, a B.Tech Computer Science student at Centurion University of Technology and Management."

problem This is the only part visible before "see more." It's pure introduction — name and university — nothing that makes someone stop scrolling.

why it hurts You've spent your single most valuable line of real estate on information that's already in your profile header, one line above.

invisible cost Most viewers never click "see more." If line 1-2 doesn't hook them, everything in paragraphs 2-4 (your actual skills and drive) goes unread.

03About — full section — 5/10

Genuinely, the effort is there — clear, honest, grammatically solid. The problem is it reads like every other CS student's About section.

"I am passionate about technology and continuously exploring opportunities to learn, build, and grow..."

problem "Passionate about technology," "solving problems," "grow into a skilled professional" — these are the three most-used phrases on student LinkedIn profiles. They say nothing a reader can act on.

why it hurts There's no proof. No project named, no outcome, no number, no link. A recruiter or peer can't tell what you've actually built vs. what you hope to build.

invisible cost You mention Python, JavaScript, SQL, Flutter, Git — good raw material — but buried in a paragraph, not surfaced as scannable proof. Someone skimming for 8 seconds misses all of it.

04Experience — 2/10

"intern in Brain O Vision as Cloud and DevOps Engineer intern" — no bullets, no dates, no scope

problem This is the weakest part of the profile right now. There's a title and a company, and that's it — no bullet points describing what you actually did.

why it hurts Experience is what a recruiter checks first after the headline. An entry with zero bullets reads as "internship exists on paper" rather than "I did real work here."

invisible cost Every keyword a bullet point could carry (AWS, Docker, CI/CD, Linux, monitoring — whatever you actually touched) is currently missing from your searchable profile.

05Skills & keywords — 3/10

problem Based on what you shared — Python basics, cloud basics, AI/API projects — your listed skills are broad and unranked, without endorsable specifics like "AWS," "Linux," "REST APIs," or "Git."

why it hurts LinkedIn's search algorithm and recruiter filters match on specific skill tags. "Cloud" alone won't surface you for "AWS," "Azure," or "GCP" searches — you need the named tools.

invisible cost You're likely invisible in recruiter searches for the exact tools you're learning, simply because they're not named as discrete skills.

The honest read: you have real substance (an active DevOps internship, multi-domain curiosity, hands-on tools) that your profile is currently hiding instead of selling. This is a positioning problem, not a substance problem — which is good news, because it's fixable in a day.
